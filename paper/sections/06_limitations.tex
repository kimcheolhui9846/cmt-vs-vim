\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The factorial experiment trains on Tiny-ImageNet: two hundred classes at
$64 \times 64$, three seeds per cell. Nothing here shows that the structure
effect keeps its size at ImageNet scale and full resolution, and the
literature it speaks to was measured there. What the experiment does support
is a comparison among four models trained identically, which is what a
transferred ablation table does not provide at any scale.

The resolution is not neutral between the two operators. Sixty-four tokens is
short enough that the quadratic term of attention is not yet expensive, which
is the regime least favourable to a linear-time scan---as
Section~\ref{sec:complexity} measures directly, the cross-over is several
hundred tokens away. Running the flat cells at $256$ tokens would put the
comparison on the other side of it. We priced it: about three hundred and
twelve GPU-hours for the twelve runs, against the \HoursTotal{} the present
campaign took. It is the single most valuable measurement not in this paper.

Our structure factor bundles two changes that could be separated. Hierarchical
downsampling and convolutional locality arrive together in cells C and D, and
no measurement here says which of them carries the effect. Separating them is
a $2\times2\times2$ design at twenty-four runs.

Every measurement comes from one consumer GPU with eight gibibytes of memory.
The throughput and memory claims we could not reproduce were reported at a
higher resolution on different hardware, and our failure to reproduce them
cannot be separated from that. We report it as a bound on where the reported
result has been shown to hold, not as a contradiction of it.

Three cells of the resolution sweep have no throughput value: CMT-S at
$512^2$ and $1024^2$, and Vim-S at $512^2$. The maximum-batch search
exhausted memory in those cells while every other measurement in the same run
completed, so the row's status is recorded as complete and only the
throughput column is empty; the reason is in the error column of the
measurement file. Table~\ref{tab:e1} marks the gaps and the figure does not
draw across them, but a reader recomputing from the file should know that the
absence is a search failure and not a zero.

Finally, the three architectures expose no structurally identical layer at
which to probe internal behaviour, which bounds how finely the
receptive-field and coverage results can be compared across models. We state
that bound where those results are used rather than only here.
