\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Comparisons between CNN--transformer hybrids and state space vision backbones
are made quantitatively. They are made with cross-over points, ratios of
floating-point operations, throughput multiples, memory reductions, and
component-wise decompositions of an accuracy gap. The numbers are specific,
and specificity is what makes them useful to a reader deciding which family
to build on.

Most of them are not measured by the text that uses them. Some are values
reported in the original papers, quoted into a setting the original authors
did not measure. Others are assertions about mechanism---about what a
one-dimensional scan does to a receptive field, or about what the absence of
a normalisation constraint does to large objects---that read as though a
measurement stands behind them, and none does. Our own earlier comparison of
these two architectures made two claims of the second kind.\footnote{An
earlier and shorter version of this comparison appeared as ``An In-Depth
Comparative Analysis of CMT and Vision Mamba'' (in Korean), paper GEP-0563,
2026 IEIE Summer Conference, poster P4-44. The present paper shares its
subject but none of its text, and reports measurements that did not exist at
that time.}

This paper takes six such claims and measures each of them. The environment
is pinned and enforced, every number in the manuscript is generated from
committed data, and every prediction was registered before the measurement
that tested it. Table~\ref{tab:verdicts} gives the outcome.

Four of the six do not hold in the form they circulate in. That could be
reported as a list of corrections, and it would be the least interesting
thing to do with it. In three of the four the argument behind the claim is
sound and the number beside it is about something else---a kernel rather than
a block, a skirt rather than a core, a normalisation rather than a capacity.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} makes that pattern explicit, because it is the
part that outlives the two architectures.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We re-measure six circulating quantitative claims
in one pinned environment and adjudicate each, separating what the original
papers reported from what comparison writing asserted. We build a
hierarchical state space backbone so that structure and token-mixing operator
can be crossed in a $2\times2$ factorial design, replacing an ablation table
transferred from one architecture to a comparison between two. And we make
the manuscript's numbers mechanically traceable: tables and in-line
quantities are generated from committed measurement files, and a test fails
the build if a measurement is typed into the prose by hand.
